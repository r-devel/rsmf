## Activities with potentially high environmental cost

### Fuzz testing of R

- Heather and Kevin discussed this with the help of ChatGPT and Claude.

- Heather's questions generated with ChatGPT:
    
    - Was the fuzz target narrowly scoped?
    
    - Was the campaign run for hours, days, or continuously?

    - How many CPU-hours were consumed?

    - Were sanitizers being used?

   -  Was the corpus reused and minimised?

   - Did additional fuzzing continue after the rate of useful discoveries had fallen substantially?

- Kevin's Claude-generated report on the energy budget currently required for a fuzzing campaign: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/c502662c-2c83-4b10-b231-25bf1420eef5. The short version is that, in its current form, the computational cost is quite small, especially compared to the world of CI infrastructure/workflows that currently exist. To answer the questions specifically:

    - The fuzz targets are narrowly scoped, specifically to components of R that are known to handle untrusted user input or are historically known to potentially have vulnerabilities.

    - The campaign currently runs for an hour a day; this will increase once it becomes part of the Google OSS-Fuzz infrastructure.
    
    - The estimate is between 10kWh and 30kWh of CPU usage per year in the current form, but this also will increase when run as part of OSS-Fuzz.

    - Sanitizers are being used, which improves the ability of the fuzzing campaigns to identify potential issues.
    
    - The corpus is reused and minimized following each run.

    - The number of findings has currently plateaued, but this is somewhat expected given the relatively minimal amount of computational resources being allocated for fuzzing right now.

- Google also maintains their own environmental report here, which discusses their efforts to reduce the environmental impact from their usage of computational resources: https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/sustainability/2026-environmental-report/
